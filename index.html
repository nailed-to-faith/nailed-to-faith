NailedToFaith — Proposal

    ⭐ Refined Proposal for the NailedToFaith Platform
A community‑built Christian space for music, testimony, teaching, and decentralised fellowship.

1. Introduction — Why This Platform Exists
The modern internet is loud, crowded, and controlled by corporations. Christian voices often feel drowned out, restricted, or forced to fit into systems that don’t reflect our values. Many believers self‑censor online because they fear judgement, algorithms, persecution, or being misunderstood.

I believe we can build something different.

NailedToFaith is a simple, independent Christian platform created to share the gospel through music, testimony, teaching, and community. It begins as a small website, but the long‑term vision is a decentralised Christian network — one where the community writes the guidelines, hosts the mirrors, and shapes the future.

This is not a corporation.
This is not a business.
This is a ministry built by ordinary believers.

2. Who This Platform Is For
A. Open to All Christian Denominations
NailedToFaith welcomes:

Protestants

Catholics

Orthodox

and all Trinitarian believers

If you believe in the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit as One, you are welcome here.

I grew up in Birmingham during the 70s and 80s, surrounded by sectarian violence. My husband served in Northern Ireland and still carries the scars. We are never going back to that. Ever.

Inter‑denominational fighting is unchristian and will not be tolerated.
We all sin.
Christ teaches us to love the sinner, not the sin.
We are not each other’s judge — Christ will resolve our differences when He returns.

B. Open to Everyone — No Login, No Tracking, No Data Collection
The Church is one of the last places on earth where you can walk in without showing ID, giving your address, or even using your real name. Anyone can join a Sunday service.

This platform reflects that same openness.

No email required

No login

No tracking

No analytics

No data collection

Faith should be accessible to all — including those who must remain anonymous for safety.

D. A Path Toward Decentralisation
Not everyone understands decentralisation, so here is the simple version:

Censorship resistance: No single company can silence the platform.

Immutability: Content can be mirrored and preserved even if one server disappears.

No overlords: Only God, not corporations, controls the platform’s future.

Underground support: Simple HTML pages can be copied to USB sticks and shared offline, reaching persecuted Christians where the internet cannot.

Resilience: Like the Pirate Bay, which reappeared within days after being taken down, decentralised Christian content can survive attempts to suppress it.

This is a slow, organic process — but it begins now.
